A hundred and fifty star systems is not an empire. It is roughly the largest set of worlds a civilization could hold in any social coherence, the interstellar version of the tribe you can know. The name has to reflect that scale, and it has to survive two hundred years of human mouths. Here is how the name was built.

## Name the relationship, not the count

Polities name themselves by their organizing principle, never by headcount. There are nearly two hundred countries and no one calls the order "the Two Hundred." They say United **Nations**, European **Union**, the **Commonwealth**. So the polity here is **the Multitude**: the mass of humanity scattered across the worlds, bound loosely, governed lightly. Anti-imperial by construction.

## Flip the lingua franca

Most space opera assumes Chinese or English as the future common tongue. This setting uses **Basque**. The word for the polity is **Jendaldea**, from *jende* (people) plus *-aldea* (group, region). It already means "crowd" or "multitude" in Basque, so the meaning is native, not coined.

## Keep the bloodline

Basque borrowed *jende* from Latin *gentem*, a people or clan. Traced into Esperanto, the same root lands on *gento*. That gives a second in-world tongue its own cognate forms: **Gentaro** (the collected peoples) and **Gentujo** (their domain). One civilization, two naming traditions, the same genetic DNA.

## Let the dialects speak

Basque *J* varies by region. In the west it is a hard H, so *Jendaldea* sounds like **Hendaldea**. In the east it softens to a Y, **Yendaldea**. A character's pronunciation places them, core or rim, before they say anything else.

## Make spelling tell time

English-substrate speakers read the *J* as an English J and say "Jen." Over generations they naturalize the spelling to match, **J becomes G**, and the modern standard becomes **Gendaldea**. The sound never changed, since Jen and Gen are identical in English. Only the spelling did. The older Basque pronunciations, Hen and Yen, survive in heritage pockets that never passed through the English mouth.

The result is a date stamp. A text that writes *Jendaldea* is founding-era or conservative. A text that writes *Gendaldea* is established and modern. Spelling alone fixes a document in time and in social space.

## The stack

- **Concept:** the Multitude
- **Charter form:** *Jendaldea* (dialect-variable, early era)
- **Modern brand:** *Gendaldea* (naturalized standard, the everyday label)
- **Esperanto cognates:** *Gentaro*, *Gentujo* (the second tongue)

Etymology, dialect, and chronology, all coherent, each in its own layer.
